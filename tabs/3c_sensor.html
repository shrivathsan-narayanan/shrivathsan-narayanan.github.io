Optimal wind sensor sensor location on multi-rotor UAVs for meteorological applications

Meteorological observations using unmanned aerial vehicles are becoming increasingly common for robust, localized measurements of atmospheric quantities. However, optimizing critical parameters such as instrumentation positioning, real-time data transfer, and vehicle flight stability remains a major challenge for effective deployment and realization. The present work investigates the aerodynamic interference in typical UAV-scale multi-rotor systems across flight conditions, and proposes optimal wind sensor placement locations based on quiet zones identified in the flow field, where rotor-wake influences are low.

A free-vortex wake method (FVM) model was utilized to perform parametric simulation of UAV-scale single, coaxial, quad- and hexa-rotor configurations across flight conditions. A supporting experimental test rig was utilized to validate the induced velocity calculations in the FVM model.
